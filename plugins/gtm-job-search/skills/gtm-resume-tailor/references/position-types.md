# Position-Type Profiles

Eight profiles used by `gtm-resume-tailor`. Load only the section matching the classified posting.

**Status: working hypotheses.** These profiles are written from general hiring practice and the position-type taxonomy in `gtm-jobs-tracker` §4a. They have not been validated against real postings and real hiring-manager feedback. Before trusting a profile, test it against at least 3 real postings and 2 real resumes for that type, and revise.

All figures in examples are placeholders in [brackets]. Never copy them into a real resume.

---

## 1. GTM Engineering

**Signal words:** GTM Engineer, Revenue Engineer, GTM Systems Engineer, GTM Architect.
**Hiring lens:** Can this person build working revenue systems and agents, and prove they run?
**Lead metrics:** hours or cost saved, pipeline or meetings generated by a system they built, data coverage or enrichment match rate, volume handled (records, accounts, sequences), reliability (error rate, uptime).
**Evidence to surface:**
- Named systems built (enrichment pipelines, routing, scoring, outbound automation, internal agents), what each did and who used it.
- Technical depth: languages, APIs, webhooks, SQL, orchestration and workflow tools, CRM and sales-engagement integrations.
- Shipped versus prototype. Production systems with users outrank demos.
- Public proof: GitHub, a demo, a write-up.
**Skills order:** Systems & Automation → Languages & Data → GTM Platforms (CRM, sequencing, enrichment) → AI/LLM tooling.
**Red flags:** a tool list with no outputs; "used [tool]" with no description of what was built; no sign of code or technical depth; "prompt engineering" as the only AI claim.
**Gap questions:** "What is the most complex workflow you built, end to end?" "What broke in production and how did you fix it?" "Who used it and what changed for them?"
**Illustrative rewrite:**
- Before: "Worked on automations for the sales team."
- After: "[N] hours/week of rep research removed by building a [N]-step enrichment and routing workflow across [CRM] and [tool], serving [N] reps with [N]% match rate."
**Level notes:** Engineer = ships systems independently. Architect, Lead and above = owns stack design, sets standards, enables others.

## 2. AI Product Management

**Signal words:** AI Product Manager, AI Product Lead, Product Manager (AI/ML).
**Hiring lens:** Has this person shipped an AI product surface and owned its quality, cost and adoption, not just the roadmap?
**Lead metrics:** adoption and retention of the AI feature, task success rate, eval scores, cost per request or per task, latency, revenue influenced.
**Evidence to surface:**
- AI features shipped, from problem to launch, and what changed after launch.
- Evaluation: how quality was defined and measured, how regressions were caught.
- Tradeoff decisions: model choice, quality versus cost versus latency, build versus buy.
- Failure-mode ownership: safety, hallucination handling, trust and fallback design.
- Working relationship with ML or engineering teams.
**Skills order:** AI Product (evals, model tradeoffs, safety) → Product Craft (discovery, roadmap, prioritization) → Data & Analytics → Tools.
**Red flags:** "added AI" with no description of what the AI does; no evaluation story; no owned metric; AI only in the company description.
**Gap questions:** "How did you decide the feature was good enough to ship?" "What was the cost or latency constraint and how did you handle it?" "What went wrong after launch?"
**Illustrative rewrite:**
- Before: "Launched AI assistant for customers."
- After: "[N]% task success rate on [N] eval cases reached before launch of an LLM-based [feature]; kept cost under $[N] per task by [decision]; [N]% weekly adoption in [N] weeks."
**Level notes:** For GTM-mandated roles, show revenue linkage. Senior and above: portfolio ownership, strategy, team influence.

## 3. Product Marketing

**Signal words:** Product Marketing Manager, PMM, Product Marketer.
**Hiring lens:** Does this person own positioning and launches with commercial results?
**Lead metrics:** pipeline from launches, win rate versus named competitors, sales cycle change, adoption, revenue influence.
**Evidence to surface:** positioning ownership, named GTM motion, launch outcomes, sales enablement impact, competitive program results, AI product positioning where the posting is AI-related.
**Skills order:** PMM Specialties → GTM & Commercial → Tools (AI PMM tier first for AI-native targets).
**Red flags:** coordination language ("helped", "supported"); launches with no outcomes; generic summary phrases.
**Gap questions:** "What number moved after the launch?" "Which competitor did you position against and what changed in win rate?"
**Level notes:** PMM ladder (PMM → Senior → Lead → Principal → Director → VP). For the full audit, Director+ benchmark and story-arc work, use a dedicated PMM resume-audit skill (not bundled with this plugin).

## 4. RevOps

**Signal words:** Revenue Operations, RevOps, Sales Operations, GTM Operations.
**Hiring lens:** Does this person make the revenue engine measurable and predictable at scale?
**Lead metrics:** forecast accuracy, conversion or cycle-time improvement, data completeness, process adoption, ARR or headcount supported.
**Evidence to surface:**
- CRM architecture and process design, not just administration.
- Forecasting, territory and quota planning, comp-plan support.
- Funnel instrumentation and reporting that changed a decision.
- Cross-team alignment between sales, marketing and customer success.
- Automation built (marks the Bridge or AI GTM track).
**Skills order:** Revenue Process & Planning → Systems (CRM, forecasting, BI) → Data & Analytics → Automation/AI.
**Red flags:** reporting only; tool administration with no business outcome; no scale stated.
**Gap questions:** "What decision changed because of a report or process you built?" "How large was the team or ARR you supported?"
**Illustrative rewrite:**
- Before: "Managed Salesforce and built dashboards."
- After: "Forecast accuracy improved from [N]% to [N]% for a $[N]M pipeline by redesigning [process] and instrumenting [stage] in [CRM], used weekly by [N] managers."
**Level notes:** Analyst/Manager = executes. Director and above = owns GTM planning, operating cadence and tooling strategy.

## 5. Forward-Deployed Engineer (FDE)

**Signal words:** Forward-Deployed Engineer, Forward Deployed GTM Engineer, Deployment Engineer, Implementation Engineer (AI/agentic product).
**Hiring lens:** Can this person get the product working inside a real customer's environment and keep the customer happy while doing it?
**Lead metrics:** time to first value or go-live, number of deployments, customer ARR deployed, adoption after launch, escalations resolved, CSAT or NPS.
**Evidence to surface:**
- Customer deployments delivered: customer type, complexity, integrations built, timeline.
- Customer-facing communication: workshops, executive updates, requirements gathering.
- Product feedback loop: issues escalated and fixes that shipped as a result.
- Technical stack and environments worked in.
**Distinguishing test (versus GTM Engineering):** deploys and configures the product inside individual customer environments, usually after the sale.
**Skills order:** Deployment & Integration → Technical Stack → Customer Engagement → Tools.
**Red flags:** purely internal engineering with no customer exposure; support work described as deployments; no timelines or outcomes.
**Gap questions:** "How long from kickoff to go-live, and what slowed it?" "Describe the hardest customer environment you worked in." "What did you push back to product, and what shipped?"
**Illustrative rewrite:**
- Before: "Helped customers implement our platform."
- After: "[N] enterprise deployments taken live in [N] weeks average (target [N]), integrating [systems] and reaching [N]% weekly active use within [N] days."
**Level notes:** Senior and above = owns multi-customer programs and feeds product direction.

## 6. Marketing Engineering

**Signal words:** Product Marketing Engineer, Technical PMM, Marketing Engineer, Growth & Marketing Engineer.
**Hiring lens:** Can this person ship the tooling and systems behind marketing and tie them to pipeline?
**Lead metrics:** conversion lift, traffic or pipeline from tools or sites built, page speed, experiment volume and win rate, cost per acquisition change, time saved.
**Evidence to surface:**
- Things shipped: sites, tools, calculators, integrations, attribution or analytics pipelines, experimentation infrastructure.
- Code and stack: languages, frameworks, APIs.
- Marketing outcome attached to each build.
- Collaboration with marketing and engineering teams.
**Skills order:** Engineering & Stack → Marketing Systems (analytics, attribution, experimentation) → Content & Positioning → Tools.
**Red flags:** marketer who "worked with developers"; developer with no marketing outcome; builds with no usage numbers.
**Gap questions:** "What did you build that marketing could not have done without you?" "What number moved because of it?"
**Illustrative rewrite:**
- Before: "Built landing pages and tracking."
- After: "[N]% conversion lift across [N] landing pages by building a [stack] template system with built-in experiment tracking, cutting launch time from [N] days to [N]."
**Level notes:** judged on scope of systems owned and the commercial result tied to them.

## 7. AEO / GEO (Answer and Generative Engine Optimization)

**Signal words:** AEO, Answer Engine Optimization, AI Search, GEO, Generative Engine Optimization.
**Hiring lens:** Can this person measurably improve a brand's presence in AI-generated answers, and explain how?
**Lead metrics:** mention or citation share across AI answer engines, referral traffic from AI search, pipeline attributed to AI-search sources, results of controlled content or structured-data experiments.
**Evidence to surface:**
- Measurement method: which engines, which prompts, how often tracked, how results were compared over time.
- Interventions: content restructuring, entity and structured-data work, source and citation strategy, with before/after results.
- Tooling used for tracking, named honestly.
- Link between AI visibility and commercial outcome, where one exists.
**Skills order:** AEO/GEO Methods & Measurement → Content & Technical SEO → Analytics → Tools.
**Red flags:** SEO relabeled with no AI-specific method; claims with no measurement; inflated tenure (the discipline is young, so a short honest history is normal; a long invented one is a disqualifier).
**Gap questions:** "How did you measure visibility in AI answers, and how often?" "What was your best controlled experiment, and what changed?" "Did any of it connect to leads or revenue?"
**Illustrative rewrite:**
- Before: "Optimized content for AI search."
- After: "Citation share in [engine] answers for [N] priority queries rose from [N]% to [N]% in [N] weeks after restructuring [N] pages and adding [structured data]; tracked weekly against [N] competitors."
**Level notes:** Early in the discipline, seniority comes from method and measurement ownership, not years.

## 8. AI Transformation

**Signal words:** AI Transformation, Transformation Lead, Head of AI Transformation, CX/GTM Strategy Manager (AI Transformation).
**Hiring lens:** Has this person actually changed how a team or organization works with AI, and measured the change?
**Lead metrics:** adoption rate, hours or cost saved, number of teams or people enabled, time to adoption, quality or revenue effect of redesigned workflows.
**Evidence to surface:**
- Rollouts completed: what tools or processes, which teams, how many people.
- Process redesign: before and after workflow, and the measured effect.
- Enablement: training, playbooks, champions programs, and adoption afterwards.
- Governance and risk: policies, data handling, approvals.
- Stakeholder management: who resisted, how it was resolved.
**Skills order:** Change & Operating Model → AI Tooling & Governance → Enablement & Training → Analytics.
**Red flags:** strategy decks with no rollout; adoption claims with no numbers; "AI strategy" with no implemented outcome.
**Gap questions:** "What changed in daily work for how many people?" "How was adoption measured at 30 and 90 days?" "What did you do about the team that did not adopt?"
**Illustrative rewrite:**
- Before: "Led AI adoption initiative across the company."
- After: "[N]% of [N] GTM staff using [workflow] weekly within [N] days after redesigning [process] and running [N] enablement sessions; saved [N] hours/week across [N] teams."
**Level notes:** Usually senior or leadership. Judged on scale of change delivered and executive stakeholder ownership.
